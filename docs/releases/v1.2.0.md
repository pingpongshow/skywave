# Skywave v1.2.0 — Release Notes

A receiver and link-adaptation release that closes the three gaps
measured against real HF conditions: fading robustness, frequency
tolerance once connected, and the intermittent kill/resume reconnect.
Every change is receive-side or local link policy: **`kWireVersion` and
`kProtocolVersion` are unchanged (both 1), and v1.2.0 interoperates with
v1.1.0 and v1.0.0.** The full investigation, root causes R1–R7 and
per-step gates are in [GAP_CLOSURE_PLAN.md](../GAP_CLOSURE_PLAN.md) and
ROADMAP Phase 8.

## What changed, and why

### Kill/resume: audio was being lost, not decoded late (R7)

In exact digital silence — a virtual cable whose peer is dead, a muted
interface — the chirp detector folded zero-energy windows into its noise
floor, which decayed toward zero; after ~30 s it reported a phantom
preamble every 0.65 s. Each phantom climbed the 90 s capture ladder, the
worker fell behind real time, the 1 s RX ring overran **silently**, and
handshake frames were destroyed (82–195 s of audio lost per station in
the archived failure). The detector now ignores zero-energy windows,
the worker drains audio before spending its decode budget, the RX ring
is 8 s, and overruns are counted, warned and reported at shutdown
(`rx overrun=N samples`). The v1.1.0 lead ("decode-latency race on
START") is refuted.

### Fading: four receiver bugs, not channel physics (R1, R4–R6)

- **CFO branch errors (R1).** Two-path multipath biased the chirp's
  coarse CFO by several ambiguity branches — the entire CCIR Poor
  outage on WIDE (previously misread as in-frame decorrelation). Every
  branch within ±55 Hz is now scored by a ZC fit and the header decode
  arbitrates.
- **Weak early path (R4).** The FFT window backs off ahead of the first
  significant path, as far as the measured delay spread leaves CP room,
  and the tracker's delay grid starts five taps earlier.
- **Kalman process noise (R5)** scales with the frame-mean channel
  power, not the power at sync time.
- **Smoothing (R6).** Frames are buffered, so every symbol is now
  equalized with a fixed-interval RTS smoother (past and future pilots).

### Frequency tolerance (R1–R3)

- Every frame signature decodes anywhere within **±50 Hz**. v1.1.0's
  fast frames captured only ±3.1 Hz (the documented ±5 Hz never
  existed), so a 5 Hz retune or 0.02 Hz/s of drift killed a session.
- An in-frame FLL tracks LO drift (≥ 1 Hz/s on OFDM modes).
- TX alignment: clamp ±30 → ±50 Hz, no slew limit, reset at every
  session end (a stale shift corrupted the next session's bootstrap),
  and the responder bootstraps from the uncorrected CALL. A 45 Hz
  mistune now converges to a 0.0 Hz residual; `EVT:CFO_WARN` can fire.
  PROTOCOL.md's "split correction" never existed and is corrected.

### Link adaptation on re-measured floors (8.9)

All floor tables were re-measured on the new receiver: AWGN 1.5–3 dB
lower, designated fading 2–7 dB lower, and WIDE/MID/ULTRA M13 gained a
fading floor. The Moderate and Severe channel classes now select
**measured per-channel tables** (`kModerateFloors`, `kPoorFloors`)
instead of the v1.1.0 caps (M8 / M7), whose premise — "the fast tier
never closes there" — no longer holds. Classifier pilot thresholds are
per family and re-measured; the SNR calibration moved 0.5 dB.

## Measured results (v1.1.0 → v1.2.0)

SNR (3 kHz ref) at 10% one-shot FER, bertool, 100 trials per point
(`docs/conformance/gap_base/` vs `gap_final/`):

| Channel / mode | v1.1.0 | v1.2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| CCIR Poor, WIDE M4 / M6 / M8 | never / never / never | −4.3 / 0.4 / 13.3 dB |
| CCIR Poor, MID M4 | never | −8.1 dB |
| CCIR Moderate, WIDE M8 / M11 | never / never | 10.4 / 21.0 dB |
| CCIR Moderate, MID M8 | 23.5 dB | 5.9 dB |
| Static CFO, fast frames | fail beyond ±3.1 Hz | ≤ 5% FER to ±50 Hz |
| LO drift 1 Hz/s, WIDE/MID | 100% FER | 0% |

Every fading ladder row improved; no AWGN point regressed. ST2 one-way
bulk (400 sim-s): 45 Hz at connect 0 B → 132 KB, 40 Hz mid-session step
dropped → 136 KB, 0.1 Hz/s drift dead at 39 s → 132 KB. Soak with
v1.2 receiver, old vs new tables: churn +15%, CCIR Poor +15–36%.

## Known limitations

- **CSS deep tier under LO drift (8.8, re-planned).** NARROW/MID M1–M3
  CSS payloads fail from ~0.05 Hz/s of drift (chirp delay-Doppler
  coupling makes SF8 pilots mis-time SF5–SF7 payload symbols). Two
  designs were measured and withdrawn because they hurt fading channels;
  data and the next design are in ROADMAP 8.8. WIDE CSS is unaffected;
  v1.1.0 had the same limit.
- **One-way flows after a mid-session retune** re-align the data
  direction only; the reverse direction keeps its offset (decoded by the
  ±55 Hz acquisition). Fixing it needs a DATA-borne CFO report
  (wire change) or a guarded reciprocity rule.
- **Extreme in-frame drift:** NARROW M4 at 1 Hz/s (10–13% FER: a 40 s
  frame) and MID M4 at 2 Hz/s.
- **No on-air validation yet.** All results are simulator and
  virtual-audio-device measurements; ST4 (attended two-radio test) is
  next.
- **The AMC does not yet capture the new receiver's gain on CCIR Good
  ramps.** On the M3 ramp it delivers the same absolute throughput as
  v1.1.0 (96.8 vs 97.5 KB, 6-seed mean) while a mode-pinned oracle shows
  15% more is available (122 vs 106 KB): it promotes into M12 at ~20 dB,
  where M12 runs ~20% one-shot FER on Good, and the failure memory then
  holds it a rung low. AMC tuning item, after on-air data.
- **ST3 soak cycle rate 12% below the v1.1.0 run** (4.03 vs 3.58 s per
  connect/transfer cycle on a clean virtual-audio link); not yet
  investigated - candidates are the per-frame decode cost of RTS
  smoothing and the CFO branch search.
- **No LICENSE file** (owner's deliberate hold, RELEASE.md gate 7):
  blocks public distribution, not internal use.

Deviations recorded during the gates (ROADMAP 8.10):

- `rel_good_narrow.csv` failed one point at 40 trials (NARROW M5 at
  floor+1, 11/40 = 27.5%). Re-measured at 400 trials: 14.0% at floor+1,
  7.2% at floor+3 — the floor stands; the archived sweep is the 200-trial
  re-run, which passes.
- `test_m3_ramp` failed its single-seed gate (seed 4200: 0.70 vs 0.75).

  demote margins were swept (−1.5…0.0 dB) and left at −1.5/−3.5: no
  margin beat it on the churn soak, and the ramp means differ by less
  than seed noise.
